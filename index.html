The gap

The procedure changed in May. Can you prove who followed which one?

01

The paper binder.

Signed sheets with no version on them.

Staff tick boxes on a printed form. A year later, nobody can say which copy of the procedure sat on the clipboard.

  • Binders
  • Sign-off sheets
  • Scans

02

The change email.

Sent to everyone. Read by some.

A new version goes out as an attachment. One shift opens it, another keeps the old printout, and the log can't tell them apart.

  • Email
  • Attachments
  • Memos

03

Document control.

Keeps every version. Records none of the work.

It knows version 4.2 replaced 4.1 on the 3rd. It cannot tell an inspector which one Tuesday's batch was made under.

  • Document systems
  • PDFs
  • Approvals

Access and audit

A request without scope never reaches the records.

Quillery checks every read, edit and tool action against the scope of the person or bot asking. Allowed requests go through, the rest stop at the gate, and each one prints a line in the audit log.

  • Scopes by role and site
  • Blocked edits stay blocked
  • An audit line per request

Sites

Where inspections are part of the week.

Food production

Allergen and sanitation records.

A line switches from a nut product to a nut-free one. The crew follows the changeover steps on a tablet, and Quillery files the record with the version, the crew and the swab result before the line restarts.

Outpatient clinics

Room turnover and sterilization.

Nurses log each instrument cycle and room clean as they finish it. When the infection control procedure changes, every record from that hour on points to the new version.

Testing labs

Sample handling and calibration.

Each sample builds a chain of records from intake to report. An assessor can pick any result and see the method version used and who calibrated the instrument that morning.
